# Accepted facts

- Successful completion requires a genuine EggCC compiler bug found using the encodings or a reproducible measured speedup. An equivalent implementation, an optimization without measured benefit, and an unsuccessful search do not satisfy the goal.
- Prefer injectivity and disequality together, but either encoding can qualify when it materially contributes to the demonstrated bug or speedup.
- A qualifying bug pre-exists the new integration in a pinned EggCC baseline and is demonstrated by a valid minimized input, an independently checked wrong result or violated semantic invariant, a localized cause and nearby passing controls.
- Repeating the completed multiplication-overflow finding or structural type-check integration does not qualify. Planted faults, unsupported or corrupted internal-only inputs, unrelated tooling failures and defects introduced by the new integration are diagnostic evidence rather than the required compiler bug.
- A qualifying speedup improves generated-program execution or end-to-end compilation on the same valid workload and matched configuration, with correct outputs, repeated measurements, explicit timing boundaries and independent confirmation beyond observed variability.
- Demonstrate the encoding contribution through the actual compiler consumer with fixed-input feature controls and a fair existing or hand-written comparison. Record discovery chronology and do not attribute an independently found bug to the encoding without evidence of its role.
- Justify each constructor and disequality law for its actual types, contexts, feasible paths, effects and refinement behavior. Do not reuse rejected global Expr, generic Concat, mutable-analysis or static-allocation assumptions without a new sound argument.
- Allow bounded compiler representations, scoped analysis, integration and encoding improvements needed by a concrete candidate. Avoid a wholesale IR redesign, new proof support, Lean work, paper edits, commits and pushes.
- Implemented candidates have registered nonzero tests, an independent oracle, positive and negative boundary cases and actual native compiler coverage. Unknown, unsupported, infeasible and missing-evidence outcomes are explicit; interpretation outputs are checked independently.
- Preserve prior studies, frozen evidence and author changes. Use isolated source changes and fresh evidence, retaining exact input, revision, dependency, patch, binary, command, configuration and machine-readable result identities.
- Report per-workload performance and neutral or regressive results. Separate compiler time from generated-program time, use the same native backend and output checks, and do not present rule-only timings, diagnostic retries or failed-versus-successful runs as a speedup.
- Test concrete bug or optimization hypotheses with early falsifiers and keep failed candidates as evidence. If the explored leads are exhausted without success, report the goal as unmet and reassess the search with the user rather than marking it complete.
- Keep memory-pressure blocking disabled as previously requested. Prefer smaller useful workloads when needed, retain timeouts and error reporting, and do not treat memory pressure alone as a reason to stop exploration.
- Deliver a collaborator-readable explanation of the successful bug or speedup, including the actual rules before and after, the connected encoding-to-consumer path, existing/manual behavior, measured results and limits, plus a compact reference and separate reproduction guide.
